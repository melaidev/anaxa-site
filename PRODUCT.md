# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Two confirmed primary audiences, reached by the same single-page site:

1. **Founders and CTOs at startups and scaleups.** Technical buyers who can evaluate
   the architecture themselves and decide without a committee. They are comparing
   Anaxa against hiring in-house or against a generalist dev shop, and they care
   whether the system will hold up in production.
2. **Operations and domain leaders at mid-market companies.** Non-technical owners of
   a business process — legal, finance, operations — who have a document or internal
   knowledge problem and no in-house AI capability. They cannot assess an architecture
   and are deciding largely on whether Anaxa seems credible and safe to hand work to.

The site must work for both without talking down to the first or over the head of the
second. The two groups need different evidence from the same page: the first reads for
technical substance, the second reads for legitimacy.

## Product Purpose

Anaxa designs and builds custom AI systems for clients — agentic workflows, retrieval
over internal knowledge, document processing, AI product development, evaluation and
reliability work, and strategy advisory. The site's job is to get a qualified visitor
to start a conversation by email. There is no product to sign up for, no trial, and no
self-serve funnel; the single conversion is the contact form.

## Positioning

Anaxa is a registered member of the **Claude Partner Network**, and every system it
builds is designed by a **Claude Certified Architect** — so the work follows Anthropic's
own best practices for building on Claude. This formal credential is the confirmed
differentiator: a neighboring consultancy can claim Claude expertise, but cannot
truthfully claim the partnership and the certification.

Consequence for all future work: the credential is the central trust asset and must be
visible and legible early, not buried in a footer. It is doing the job that client logos
and case studies would otherwise do.

## Operating Context

Visitors arrive from a link — referral, LinkedIn, a conversation — rather than from
search traffic or a campaign. Most will read the page once, in a single scroll, and
either write or leave. There is no return visit to design for, no logged-in state, and
no second page. Everything that has to be communicated has to be communicated in one
pass.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Single static page. No build step, no framework, no server; hand-authored HTML with
  inline CSS and vanilla JavaScript.
- Six service areas are confirmed product truth: Agentic Workflows, RAG & Internal
  Knowledge, Document Processing, AI Product Development, Evaluation & Reliability,
  Strategy & Advisory.
- Contact is a modal form posting to Formspree (form `mjgpgpbl`), delivering to
  `hello@anaxa.ai`. Fields: email, message, and how they heard about Anaxa.
- Domain `anaxa.ai`; DNS at GoDaddy, mail on Google Workspace.
- **Undecided:** whether the site ever grows past one page. Nothing should be built that
  assumes a second route exists.

## Brand Commitments

- **The company name is "Anaxa AI"** (confirmed 2026-10-09, superseding the earlier
  "Anaxa"). Use it everywhere the company is named, including the wordmark and the
  footer. Two exceptions: the legal entity stays **Anaxa Sdn Bhd**, and the email address
  stays `hello@anaxa.ai`. `anaxa.ai` remains the domain, not the name.
- Binding copy, confirmed verbatim by the user: *"Anaxa is a registered member of the
  Claude Partner Network. Every system we build is designed by a Claude Certified
  Architect, so it follows Anthropic's own best practices for building on Claude."*
- Existing logo mark: two stacked rounded tiles with a lowercase two-storey `a` knocked
  out of the front tile, in `brand/`. The two-storey `a` is a stated requirement — the
  single-storey circle-and-stem form was explicitly rejected.
- **Standing visual preference (confirmed 2026-10-09):** offered a replacement visual
  world, the user chose the category standard — the conventional arrangement for this
  kind of site, executed at full craft rather than replaced by a distinctive world.
  Future rounds should treat convention as the commitment, not as a fallback, and should
  not re-litigate this unless the user reopens it.
- **Craft bar (confirmed 2026-10-09):** Anthropic, OpenAI, Scale. These set the expected
  finish level — generous whitespace, serious editorial tone, research-adjacent
  credibility. This is a bar for *craft*, not an instruction to adopt their brand
  identity. Anaxa is a Claude Partner Network member, so borrowing Anthropic's actual
  palette, typefaces, or logo treatment would be both a partner-guidelines problem and a
  loss of distinct identity; match the quality, not the clothes.
- Stated negative constraints from the user, recorded because they bound future work:
  heavy/bold display weights were rejected; a cream or beige ground with an oversized
  italic serif headline was rejected; close visual resemblance to headstart.nyc was
  rejected; and the current restrained near-white minimal treatment was judged
  indistinguishable from generic AI-generated output.

## Evidence on Hand

**No public proof exists.** There are no nameable clients, no logos, no case studies, no
testimonials and no metrics that may be shown. Future work must not invent any of these,
including as placeholder or illustrative content.

What is real and usable: the Claude Partner Network membership, the Claude Certified
Architect credential, the six service areas, and the quality of the writing itself.
Credibility has to be carried by those alone.

## Product Principles

1. **The credential carries the trust load.** With no case studies, the partnership and
   certification are the only external proof. Treat them as primary content.
2. **One pass, two readers.** A technical buyer and a non-technical process owner must
   both get what they need from a single scroll, without a version for each.
3. **Claim nothing that cannot be shown.** No fabricated clients, numbers, or outcomes.
   Specificity has to come from describing the work accurately, not from invented proof.
4. **Plain words over category language.** The audience split makes jargon expensive —
   it loses the second reader and does not impress the first.
5. **Everything serves the email.** The only success event is a contact form submission.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Text contrast must meet WCAG AA (4.5:1 for body copy). The incumbent palette failed this
at 2.77:1 on secondary text and was corrected to 4.78:1; future palettes must be checked
rather than assumed.
