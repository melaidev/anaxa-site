Industrial Operations
Condition-based maintenance & anomaly detection
ConstraintReconciling inconsistent data across separate source systems, encoding domain-expert knowledge, and proving the system was reliable enough for operators to act on.

04 / How We Work

Methodology matters more than enthusiasm.

Most AI projects don't stall because the technology is wrong. They stall because the process around it — how requirements are set, how quality is measured, how systems get handed over — borrows from software norms that don't account for probabilistic outputs.

AnaxaAI has built a practice around closing that gap. Explicit evaluation criteria before writing a line of code. Documented edge cases and limits before launch. Monitoring after.

01
Define “working” before we start
Every engagement begins with evaluation criteria. What does success look like and how do we measure it? We won't build without this.
02
Treat complexity as the brief
Legacy infrastructure, regulatory constraints, messy data — these aren't obstacles. They're the actual design requirements. We work with reality as it is.
03
Full-stack accountability
From architecture and integration to deployment and post-launch monitoring, we take ownership of what we ship — no handoffs to ambiguity.
04
Safety and governance built in
Guardrails, audit trails, and access controls aren't afterthoughts. We design them in from the start, especially in health, energy, and industrial contexts.
05 / Principles

Three things we don't compromise on.

01
Ship, don't pilot

Most enterprise AI never makes it past the pilot. We design for production from day one — with the evaluation criteria, monitoring, and rollback plans that a live system actually needs.

02
Earn trust with evidence

We don't ask you to trust the AI. We hand over the eval results, benchmarks, and audit trails — so you can see for yourself where the system is reliable and where it isn't.

03
Don't oversell

We'll tell you when a problem isn't ready for AI, or when a simpler tool would do the job. Honest scoping up front costs less than a project that quietly falls apart later.
